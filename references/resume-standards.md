# Résumé writing and layout standards

Use these standards derived from the supplied updated “Resume Best Practice” slides and the supplied sample résumé. Apply the sample's layout and bullet structure. Keep example claims and contact details out of candidate drafts. When the slides suggest omitting content, preserve the item and use a review comment.

## Read the job posting

- Identify the role level, recurring duties, required skills, preferred skills, tools, and expected outcomes. Use repeated and early-listed duties to guide emphasis. Use the scoring rules in the main skill for the score.
- Mirror supported job verbs, industry terms, and tools in context. Show soft skills with examples of the work. Preserve official job titles and seniority. Add a short employer description only when the available evidence supports it.
- Prioritize recent and relevant achievements. Keep older entries and use comments for proposed cuts.

## Sample Word layout

- Use one US Letter page, one column, a white background, black text, and 0.75-inch margins by default. Use Times New Roman at 11 pt for body text. Use 10–12 pt as needed. Adjust wording, spacing, and font size before changing margins. Keep margins between 0.5 and 1 inch.
- Center the bold uppercase name at about 14 pt. Put a nickname in quotation marks when one is supplied. Place a compact center-aligned contact line beneath it at about 10 pt. Keep contact details as selectable text in the main body.
- Use compact bold section headings at about 11 pt with a thin paragraph rule, as in the sample. Use recognizable headings such as Education, Experience, and Skills & Interests. Map additional sections only if the meaning and all entries remain intact.
- Use compact line spacing and consistent bullet indents. Use Word bullets. Keep the name and each heading with the next paragraph. Avoid forced line breaks within bullets.
- Put the institution or employer in bold, then its location. Set a right-aligned tab at the right text margin for the date or date range. If the text on the left would collide with the date, wrap the left text and keep the date aligned right. Put the degree or role title in italics on the next line. Keep the sample's order within each entry. Use reverse chronology within comparable groups.
- Keep the document in natural reading order. Use body paragraphs and tabs. Avoid tables, columns, text boxes, sidebars, logos, images, graphics, and critical text in page headers or footers.

## Education

- Use full official institution and degree names when supported, such as “Master of Business Administration,” and month and year when known. Never infer a missing date component.
- Label supporting bullets, such as Honors, Membership, or Coursework. Preserve every listed degree, detail, and credential. Comment if high school is listed alongside a college degree and removal would help.

## Experience

- Lead with a strong action verb. Use past tense for completed work, including completed achievements in current roles. Use present tense where needed to preserve the meaning of ongoing responsibilities.
- Use compact situation, task, action, and result phrasing. Include context where it makes the action or result clear. Keep reported figures, scope, ownership, and timeframes. Use supported qualitative outcomes such as adoption, recognition, or a new process if no numeric result is supplied.
- Keep distinct positions and duties visible. Group titles with equivalent responsibilities under one employer only if each title and its dates stay visible and the shared bullets remain correctly attributed. Use the consulting firm as the employer and mention clients in bullets when the source supports that relationship.
- Give prominent placement to the most relevant work. Emphasize recent experience where useful and preserve earlier entries. Keep gaps as reported and never invent explanations or extend dates.

## Quantify the work

- Lead with the action. Add the scope and verified result when they help show a job requirement. Use context only where it makes the action or result clear.
- Prefer an outcome such as revenue, cost, turnaround time, quality, conversion, or adoption. When an outcome is unavailable, show scale with the team size, audience size, workload, delivery volume, or frequency. Use a relevant number to show the work behind a soft skill.
- Preserve existing figures and qualifiers. Keep the unit, baseline, time period, and contribution accurate. Use only figures supported by the résumé, applicant confirmation, or evidence the applicant supplies. Keep examples from the slides out of the résumé.
- If a key bullet lacks a useful figure, ask for a specific metric in a Word comment. For example, “If tracked, add the number of teams that adopted the tool.” Or “Add the average turnaround time before and after the change, if available.”
- When no number is available, use a supported result such as adoption, recognition, expanded scope, or a new process. Use numbers to clarify the work. Avoid fixed metric counts or word-count targets.

## Skills and interests

- Use labeled bullets for software or technical skills, languages, certifications, leadership, service, awards, or interests. Separate technical skills from languages. Include specific software capabilities and proficiency only when supported.
- Use at least one labeled bullet if the résumé contains evidence for this section. Do not add an empty section or infer a qualification to fill it.
